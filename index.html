Australia's Learning Divide

Where are Australia's literacy and numeracy gaps, and which students are being left behind?

Literacy and numeracy shape students' educational opportunities, yet achievement is not distributed equally across Australia. This visualisation explores recent NAPLAN results across school year levels, states and territories, gender and remoteness, before placing Australia's performance in an international context using OECD PISA data.

Reading the data: NAPLAN proficiency reporting changed in 2023. Trend comparisons therefore focus on results from 2023 onwards.

Key finding

Equity and opportunity

Which students face the largest gaps?

Geography is only part of the story. Achievement also differs across student groups. Gender patterns vary between Reading and Numeracy, while remoteness is associated with particularly large differences in average NAPLAN performance.

Equity matters

The strongest contrasts become visible when location and student background are considered alongside the national average.

Gender

What to notice: The gender pattern reverses: females lead in Reading, while males lead in Numeracy.

Change over time

State rankings remain relatively stable

Tracking Year 3 Reading performance over time shows that much of the jurisdictional ordering remained stable between 2023 and 2025, although some states changed position.

A stable hierarchy

Victoria remained first across the period, while movement was more visible among the middle-ranked jurisdictions.

Rankings

05

Beyond Australia

Australia in international context

NAPLAN reveals differences within Australia, while PISA provides an international benchmark. Australia's mathematics performance sits above the OECD average, but there remains a substantial spread between lower- and higher-performing students.

International perspective

Australia's average mathematics performance is above the OECD average, but the wide performance range shows why a national mean cannot describe every student's experience.

PISA 2022

What to notice: Australia's mean of about 487 exceeds the OECD average of about 472, but the distance between lower- and higher-performing students remains wide.

06

The takeaway

The national average tells only part of the story

Australia's literacy and numeracy results reveal substantial differences across geography, year level and student background. Looking only at a national score can obscure these gaps.

Understanding educational performance therefore requires attention not only to overall achievement, but also to how opportunity and outcomes are distributed across different groups and locations.
